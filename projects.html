Table Mountain sky brightness

Observational Astrophysics · Fall 2025 · final project

  • Designed a month-long observing plan to map sky brightness at Table Mountain Observatory in time and across the sky: three fixed fields (zenith and about 30° north and south, toward Los Angeles), imaged nightly in clear, g, r, and i during true night, 3 × 10 s each. Observations were taken by a classmate.
  • Reduced the images in Python: sigma-clipped master dark-bias and flat frames, calibration and alignment by field, and plate solving with astrometry.net.
  • Calibrated to mag/arcsec² with aperture photometry (aperture chosen from a growth curve at about 2× FWHM), per-field zero points from GSC 2.3 reference stars, and a sigma-clipped median sky background.
  • Compared the image-based results with the site's zenith-pointing Unihedron SQM (5-minute cadence, reduced to nightly medians) and traced field-dependent offsets to observing geometry, airmass, and the SQM's broader bandpass.

Median sky: 19.45 (zenith), 19.43 (north), 18.66 (south) mag/arcsec²
Night-to-night swings up to 1.25 mag, driven by lunar phase
Southward field 0.5–1.4 mag brighter than the SQM on every overlapping night

Tools: Python (Astropy), SAOImage DS9, astrometry.net

Calibrated sky brightness by night for the three fields. The sky is brightest at full moon (red line), and the field toward Los Angeles is brightest throughout. The gap is a storm.

Image minus SQM residuals on the four nights both were available. Negative means the image saw a brighter sky.

Fourier transform spectrometer

Optics Lab · Feb 2026 · with Sara Morishige

  • Built and aligned a Michelson-interferometer FTS to resolve the sodium doublet.
  • Fit 5 trials × 7 visibility minima in Python (SciPy) with a linear model and rejected a >4σ outlier traced to micrometer backlash.
  • Measured fringe visibility of the 546 nm mercury line with a photodiode, amplifier, and electrical filter to suppress 120 Hz lamp flicker, and diagnosed a drop in signal-to-noise between sessions by testing the photodiode and adjusting the detector aperture.

Na doublet spacing 0.593 ± 0.0004 nm, 1.2% from literature
Fit: reduced χ² = 1.69, PTE = 0.13

The FTS set up for the sodium measurement.

Micrometer position of each visibility minimum, with linear fit.

Grating spectrometer calibration

Optics Lab · Apr 2026 · with Jiani Fu

  • Calibrated a SPEX 500M scanning grating spectrometer with photomultiplier-tube readout against 11 mercury lines, fitting Lorentzian peak centers and a linear wavelength correction.
  • Flagged leftover non-linearity in the calibration (reduced χ² = 2.52, structured residuals) as a systematic error and proposed a broader-wavelength calibration to characterize it.
  • Tuned slits and focusing to record Balmer lines four orders of magnitude fainter than the 656 nm line, and resolved isotope-shifted hydrogen and deuterium lines.

Calibration error 0.014% (average)
Na spin-orbit energy 0.0021 ± 0.0002 eV
Rydberg constant within 0.14% (H), 0.06% (D)

Tools: Igor Pro, Lorentzian fitting, error propagation

Mercury-line calibration: measured vs. literature wavelength, with residuals.

Deuterium and hydrogen Balmer lines near 656 nm, separated by the isotope shift.

CCD photometry of a δ Scuti variable

Observational Astrophysics · Sep 2025 · with Gada Tefara

  • Observed remotely with the Pomona 1-meter telescope at Table Mountain Observatory.
  • Reduced 120 alternating g/r exposures: built master bias, dark, and flat frames (100 biases, 100 darks, twilight flats) with sigma-clipped combining, then calibrated and aligned the science frames.
  • Rejected frames corrupted by a tracking failure and chose a 13-pixel aperture from a growth curve to handle focus drift (mean SNR ≈ 606).
  • Calibrated light curves against six reference stars and used the color change to show temperature, not radius, drives the brightness variation.

Pulsation period 78 ± 5 min

g- and r-band light curves of δ Scuti star 1362.

Globular cluster distance from HST archival data

Observational Astrophysics · Fall 2025

  • Built a ~150,000-source catalog from archival HST/ACS images (MAST) with DAOStarFinder, and cut crowded sources with radial and background-flux filters.
  • Ran aperture photometry with zero-point, aperture (encircled-energy), and extinction corrections, then median-binned the color–magnitude diagram.
  • Fit Dartmouth isochrones and shifted the main sequence to estimate the cluster's distance.

Distance 12.6 ± 0.6 kpc

Tools: Python, Astropy, photutils, DS9

Archival HST/ACS image of the cluster.
